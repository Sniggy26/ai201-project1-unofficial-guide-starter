Georgia State University Financial Aid Guide

The Office of Student Financial Aid at Georgia State University manages grants, loans, scholarships, and work-study for students. All financial aid information is communicated via your GSU student email (Panther Mail) and your PAWS account.

How to Apply for Financial Aid at GSU:
1. Complete the FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) at studentaid.gov
2. List Georgia State University as one of your schools (GSU's federal school code is 001574)
3. Wait for GSU to receive your FAFSA results from the federal processor
4. Check your PAWS account and student email for any additional documents required
5. Accept your financial aid award package in PAWS

Accepting Your Award in PAWS:
- Go to paws.gsu.edu and log in
- Select the Finances tab
- Federal grants (like Pell Grant) are automatically accepted for you
- Federal work-study and federal loans require you to manually accept them
- Do NOT accept aid for semesters you will not be enrolled in

Types of Financial Aid at GSU:
- Federal Pell Grant: Need-based grant that does not need to be repaid. Amount depends on your FAFSA results.
- Federal Direct Loans: Must be repaid after graduation. Subsidized loans do not accrue interest while in school; unsubsidized loans do.
- Federal Work-Study: Part-time campus jobs for students with financial need
- Institutional Scholarships: GSU offers various merit and need-based scholarships
- HOPE Scholarship: Georgia residents who maintain a 3.0 GPA may qualify for the Georgia HOPE Scholarship, which covers a portion of tuition

Financial Aid Verification:
If you are selected for verification, GSU will notify you via your PAWS email. You will need to submit additional documents. Failing to submit these documents on time will delay or cancel your financial aid.

Common Financial Aid Problems:
- Missing deadlines: Financial aid has strict deadlines. Missing the FAFSA deadline can mean losing thousands of dollars in grant money.
- Getting dropped from classes: If your financial aid is not processed before the tuition deadline, you may be dropped from classes. Contact the Office of Student Financial Aid immediately if this happens.
- Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP): You must maintain satisfactory academic progress to keep your financial aid. This includes maintaining a minimum GPA and completing a minimum percentage of your attempted credit hours.

Panther Book Access Program:
GSU's Panther Book Access Program allows students to charge textbooks and course materials to their student account. The cost is included in your tuition bill, and financial aid can cover it. This is much cheaper than buying textbooks at retail price.
